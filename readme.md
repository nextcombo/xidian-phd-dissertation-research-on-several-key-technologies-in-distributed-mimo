# [Xidian PhD Dissertation] Research on Several Key Technologies in Distributed MIMO Radar Target Detection [Attached MATLAB Code]

In the increasingly complex operational environment, distributed radar systems play an increasingly important role. Distributed radar systems can not only cooperate in combat through observing different areas but also enhance detection capabilities by observing the same area. Distributed radar systems have excellent performance gains and flexible and variable working methods, making them one of the important directions for the development of modern radar technology.
The main research content of this paper is the signal-level fusion detection algorithm for distributed radar systems. Due to the different spatial scattering characteristics of targets at different spatial angles, multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) radars can utilize this characteristic to achieve spatial diversity gain, thereby improving the target detection performance of the radar system. How to reduce various adverse factors on the distributed radar system and minimize implementation costs while ensuring stable detection performance are two important issues that need to be addressed urgently in the practical application of distributed radar systems. This paper focuses on three key issues of distributed radar systems: firstly, it analyzes the impact of SNR differences on signal-level fusion detection performance and provides corresponding improvement methods to ensure good detection performance; secondly, considering the issue of large data transmission volume of signal-level fusion algorithms, it studies the dual threshold signal-level fusion detection algorithm, which significantly reduces the amount of transmitted data while ensuring good detection performance; finally, considering the inevitable misalignment error of distributed radar systems, it analyzes the impact of spatial alignment errors on signal-level fusion detection performance and proposes corresponding improvement methods to ensure the detector has good robustness against misalignment errors. The specific research content of this paper is as follows:
1. Briefly introduced the conventional signal-level fusion detection algorithm for distributed MIMO radars. First, the classical system structure, signal model, and signal processing flow were presented. Depending on whether prior SNR information is known, the traditional likelihood ratio test (LRT) detector and non-coherent accumulation (NCI) detector were derived. Compared with traditional single-baseline radars, this demonstrates the improvement in distributed radar detection performance due to spatial diversity gain. Second, the detection performance of the LRT detector and NCI detector under different channel SNR conditions was compared, analyzing the impact of prior SNR information on detection performance. Finally, a detailed evaluation of the detection performance of the distributed radar system was conducted through simulation experiments.
2. The study investigated the impact of SNR differences on the performance of distributed MIMO radar signal level fusion detection. For LRT detectors, since the SNR information is known, optimal detection performance can be achieved. This paper primarily studies the influence of SNR differences on NCI detector performance. It is easy to understand that when high SNR channels are fused with low SNR channels, there may be a reduction in system detection performance. Numerical simulation results show that under different conditions, increasing the same low SNR channel has varying effects on detection performance. In the absence of SNR information, to enable effective channel fusion and prevent the impact of low SNR channels on detection performance, this paper introduces the concept of Cumulative Contribution Rate (CCR) from statistical theory, proposing a robust signal-level fusion algorithm for SNR differences. The magnitude of SNR is reflected in local inspection statistics. Therefore, setting a CCR threshold and performing CCR calculations on the local inspection statistics can accumulate the high SNR channels. Numerical simulations reveal that by setting appropriate CCR thresholds, it is possible to estimate the number of effective channels for accumulation, improving the performance of signal-level fusion detection algorithms when there are SNR differences.
3. Research on signal-level fusion detection algorithms under data transmission rate constraints. Traditional signal-level fusion detection algorithms require the transmission of all observational data, which has a high communication requirement and increases system costs. In practical engineering applications, most of the radar echoes contain clutter and noise signals, with target signals being sparse, and the clutter and noise signals have relatively lower power compared to detectable target signals. Based on this characteristic, this paper studies the dual threshold signal-level fusion detection algorithm. Spatial diversity channels set the first threshold according to the data transmission rate requirements, and after passing through the first threshold, only data exceeding the first threshold is transmitted to the fusion center. Then, signal-level fusion detection is performed at the fusion center, comparing with the second threshold to obtain the final decision result. Through the first threshold processing, most of the low-power signals can be eliminated, retaining effective signals, thereby reducing the data transmission rate. Through experimental simulation analysis, the dual threshold signal-level fusion detection algorithm significantly reduces the data transmission rate with minimal performance loss, demonstrating its engineering application value.
4. Research on signal-level fusion detection algorithms with spatial alignment errors. This paper first theoretically analyzes the impact of spatial alignment errors in distributed MIMO radar on the detection performance of traditional LRT detectors and derives explicit expressions for false alarm probability and detection probability. When there are spatial alignment errors, in order to effectively accumulate target signal energy, this paper proposes an uncertain area-based signal-level fusion detection algorithm. In each spatial diversity channel, the position of the target echo signal in the uncertain area needs to be estimated first, and then the estimated target signal is fused. Depending on whether the prior knowledge of the alignment error distribution is known, two detection algorithms - generalized likelihood ratio and Bayesian likelihood ratio - are proposed. Through experimental simulation analysis, it is shown that alignment errors severely reduce the detection performance of signal fusion, and the two detection algorithms proposed in this paper have certain robustness against alignment errors.

The paper is divided into six chapters, with the specific content arrangement as follows:
Chapter 1 is an introduction, which first introduces the basic concepts, research significance, and current status of distributed MIMO radar and signal-level fusion detection, and briefly lists the main work of the paper.
Chapter 2 focuses on traditional distributed MIMO radar signal-level fusion detection algorithms. Firstly, it briefly describes the structural model of the distributed MIMO radar system and the basic signal processing flow. Secondly, it derives the traditional LRT detector and Non-Coherent Integration (NCI) detector; analyzes the differences between distributed and single-base station radar systems in target detection to illustrate the impact of spatial diversity gain and coherent accumulation gain on detection performance. Finally, through experimental comparisons, it analyzes the performance of LRT and NCI detectors at different channel SNRs and examines the influence of prior SNR information on detection performance.
Chapter 3 primarily investigates the impact of SNR differences on signal level fusion detection algorithms when multiple spatial diversity channels exist. For LRT detectors, since the known SNR information, optimal detection performance can be achieved. However, for NCI detectors with unknown SNR, the fusion of high and low SNR data affects detection performance. Drawing from the concept of cumulative contribution rate (CCR) in statistics [169], a robust signal level fusion algorithm that is resilient to SNR differences is studied. This method effectively eliminates low SNR data and fuses high SNR data, improving the performance of signal level fusion detection algorithms under SNR differences.
Chapter 4 mainly studies signal level fusion detection algorithms under data transmission rate constraints. In practice, most of the radar echoes received are cluttered noise signals, with target signals being sparse, and most clutter and noise signals have relatively small power compared to the target signals to be detected. Based on this characteristic, a dual-threshold signal level fusion detection algorithm is studied. Each spatial diversity channel sets a first threshold according to the data transmission rate requirements, and after passing through the first threshold, only data exceeding the threshold is transmitted to the fusion center. Then, signal level fusion detection is performed at the fusion center. Through the first threshold processing, most of the low-power signals can be eliminated, retaining effective signals, thereby reducing data transmission rate. Single pulse echo dual-threshold detection algorithms under known and unknown SCNR conditions are studied, and detector structures under different criteria are derived. Referencing the detector structure of single pulse echoes, the analysis also covers the dual-threshold detection algorithm for multi-pulse echoes. Through experimental simulation analysis, the dual-threshold signal level fusion detection algorithm significantly reduces the data transmission rate requirement without significant loss in detection performance.
Chapter 5 primarily investigates signal-level fusion detection algorithms when spatial registration errors exist. Firstly, theoretically, the impact of spatial registration errors on the performance of traditional LRT detectors is analyzed. Under certain conditions, explicit expressions for false alarm probability and detection probability are derived. In radar systems with registration errors, target echoes appear within an uncertain region, where the size of the uncertainty zone depends on the magnitude of the registration error. The larger the registration error, the greater the uncertainty zone; conversely, the smaller the registration error, the smaller the uncertainty zone. To effectively accumulate target signals within this uncertainty zone, signal-level fusion detection is implemented. Within each spatial diversity channel, the first step involves estimating the position of the target echo signal within the uncertainty zone. Subsequently, the estimated target signal is fused. When the distribution of spatial registration errors is unknown, ML estimation methods are employed to estimate the target signal; when the spatial registration error distribution is known, Bayesian estimation methods are utilized to estimate the target signal. Through experimental simulation analysis, it is evident that registration errors significantly affect the performance of signal fusion detection. The two detection algorithms proposed in this chapter demonstrate robustness against registration errors.
